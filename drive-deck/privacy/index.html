Privacy Policy

Drive Deck is an Android Auto music player by Alexei Yarilovets. This page explains what the app can see on your phone, what it keeps, and what it shares. The short answer: it collects nothing about you and sends nothing anywhere.

Effective date: 14 November 2026

In short

  • Drive Deck has no server, no account, no analytics and no ads.
  • It does not ask Android for internet access, so it cannot send anything over the network.
  • What it keeps, such as your favorites and where playback stopped, stays on your phone.
  • Nobody, including the developer, receives any data from the app.

Who is responsible

Drive Deck is developed by Alexei Yarilovets, the developer named on the Drive Deck page in Google Play. For any question about privacy, write to the developer email address shown on that page.

What the app can access

Your music folder

You pick one folder on the phone with Android's own folder picker. Drive Deck gets read-only access to that folder and its subfolders, and to nothing else. It reads the folder and file names to show them and the audio files to play them. It cannot change or delete your files.

YouTube Music, if you use it

To control YouTube Music, Drive Deck needs notification access, which you turn on yourself in Android settings. Android only lets an app with this access see the media players that are playing. Drive Deck uses it to see what YouTube Music plays (the song title, artist, album and the next songs in its queue) and to send it play, pause, previous, next and like. Drive Deck does not read, keep or share your notifications.

Permissions

The app asks Android for these permissions and no others:

  • Show its screen in Android Auto (androidx.car.app.MEDIA_TEMPLATES).
  • Keep playing in the background (FOREGROUND_SERVICE and FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK).
  • Show the playback notification (POST_NOTIFICATIONS).
  • Keep the phone awake while it plays (WAKE_LOCK).
  • See whether the phone has a network connection (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE). This tells the app only whether the phone is connected, and how; it does not let the app go online.

The last two come with Google's media library, which the app uses to play music. Android libraries also add one internal permission (DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION) that only stops other apps from sending messages to Drive Deck; you never see it, and it gives the app no access to anything.

It has no access to the internet, your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or other files.

What the app keeps, and where

Drive Deck keeps only what it needs to work, in its private storage on your phone:

  • the music folder you picked;
  • your favorite folders and playlists;
  • your liked songs: links to the files, and the titles of songs liked in YouTube Music mode;
  • the YouTube Music playlists you shared to the app: their names and links;
  • where playback stopped: the folder, the song and the position, or the playlist, the song title and the position;
  • your settings: the mode (your files or YouTube Music) and the color tone.

This data is not included in Android backups or in transfers to a new phone, and it never leaves your phone.

What the app shares

Nothing. Drive Deck does not send any data to the developer or to anyone else, and it does not sell or share data. Android Auto shows the app's screen in your car over the phone's connection to the car, as it does for every Android Auto app. Android Auto, YouTube Music and Google Play are services of Google and follow Google's Privacy Policy.

How to delete your data

  • Clear the app's storage: open Android Settings, then Apps, then Drive Deck, then Storage, and tap Clear storage. The names of these steps can differ a little between phones.
  • Or uninstall Drive Deck. Android removes everything the app kept, and its access to your music folder.
  • To take back notification access, turn Drive Deck off on the notification access page of Android settings.

Children

Drive Deck is not made for children, and it collects no data from anyone, children included.
