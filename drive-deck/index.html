An easy-to-use Android Auto music player

Large controls and a clear, simple screen: you hit what you mean without aiming, and you never need to reach for the phone.

How to try it View on GitHub

Drive Deck plays the music files on your phone, or works as a remote for YouTube Music. It runs on your Android phone and shows up on the car's screen through Android Auto.

Free and open source (MIT). No account, no analytics, no internet access.

Why it exists

I kept squinting at the buttons, trying to figure out how to pick my favorite track instead of just skipping to the next one. Every attempt meant taking my eyes off the road and staring at the screen, usually at the worst possible moment.

Using the phone itself isn't an option: tapping on it while driving is exactly what police fine you for, and fair enough, honestly.

On top of that, my music was split between two places: local MP3 and FLAC files on my phone, and YouTube Music. I wanted everything at my fingertips, not scattered across two apps with completely different interfaces.

So Drive Deck gives me four tiles, as large as Android Auto allows, with nothing to read: Previous, Play/Pause, Next, and Folder. When I want to pick something specific, Folder opens my directories or playlists, keeping my favorites just one tap away. Those exact same four tiles handle both my local files and YouTube Music. Everything stays in the same spot every time, so a quick tap is all it takes.

One screen, made for a glance

Everything you need while driving is on one screen: what plays, where it comes from, and four big buttons. Here is what you do with them.

Pick what to play

Tap Folder and pick a folder, or a playlist in YouTube Music mode. Star favorites on the phone, and the row under the song leads to them: one starts with a single tap, several open a short list.

Your files or YouTube Music

The button at the top switches between the music files on your phone and YouTube Music. The same four tiles work in both.

Create your playlist on the fly

Tap the song name and it joins your liked songs; tap again to take it back. A liked song shows a heart. LIKED in the header opens the list and plays it from the song you tap. In YouTube Music mode the like goes to YouTube Music too.

Buttons you can't miss

Previous, Play/Pause and Next are big tiles with nothing to read, always in the same place. Play/Pause looks pressed in while music plays.

Your music folder, read where it is

Pick one folder on the phone once. Drive Deck plays the MP3 and FLAC files in it and in its subfolders, straight from where they are. It does not copy or upload your music.

Drive Deck stays on the phone

Drive Deck has no server, no account and no analytics. It does not even ask Android for internet access. The details are in the Privacy Policy.

The car shows the screen. The phone does the rest.

Your music folder, Drive Deck and the YouTube Music app are all on the phone. Android Auto sends the screen to the car over USB or wirelessly, and passes your taps back. The car never reads your files.

Four permissions, none of them internet

This is all the app asks Android for. The exact names, as in its manifest, are under each line.

It may

  • Show its screens in the carMEDIA_TEMPLATES
  • Keep playing in the backgroundFOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK
  • Show the media notificationPOST_NOTIFICATIONS

It does not ask to

  • Go onlineINTERNET
  • Read all your storage or all your musicREAD_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, READ_MEDIA_AUDIO, MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE

One folder, nothing else

Android's folder picker gives Drive Deck read access to the one folder you choose, not to your whole storage. Pick a folder on the phone itself: a cloud storage app behind the picker could still go online to fetch files.

YouTube Music stays YouTube Music

In YouTube Music mode, Drive Deck is a remote. It reads what YouTube Music is playing and sends it play, pause, previous, next and like. It cannot see your library, and it downloads nothing. To read the player, it needs notification access, which you grant on Android's settings page.

Try it

In a car, Android Auto shows only apps installed from Google Play. Drive Deck is there in internal testing for now, so there are two ways to get it into your car, and one more for developers.

In your car

Join the testers

Ask to be added to the internal testing: open a tester request on GitHub. Do not post your email there; we will agree how you send the Google address to add. Then install Drive Deck from Google Play on your phone, and it shows up in your car.

Ask to join the testers

Use your own Google Play account

Have a Google Play developer account? Build Drive Deck from source under your own package name and upload it to your own internal testing. Then it installs from Google Play, and your car shows it.

Step by step: Releasing to Google Play.

On a computer, for developers

Before you start

  1. A phone with Android 8.0 or later and Android Auto.
  2. To build: Android Studio with its bundled JDK, and the Android SDK for API level 36.
  3. The DHU, the Android Auto emulator for a computer, with "Unknown sources" turned on in Android Auto's developer settings. A build you install yourself shows only there.

Step by step: Testing on the DHU.

Build from source

Clone the repository and install the debug build on your phone.

shell 
$ git clone https://github.com/zalexo/media-app.git
$ cd media-app
$ ./gradlew installDebug

The debug build installs as Drive Deck (dev), so it can sit next to a build from Google Play.
